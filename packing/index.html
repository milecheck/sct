Packing for the Sunshine Coast Trail

◆Containers for 3 litres of water. Carry the full capacity for dry camps and the Tin Hat dry stretch.
◆Water filter and backup treatment. Sources include lakes and creeks. Some are seasonal and many remain unchecked.
◆Your own food-storage setup. Some huts have no bear cache. Existing caches may be full or damaged.
◆A sleeping pad and tent. Hut platforms are first-come and may be full.
◆Weigh the loaded pack. Include food and dry-camp water. Three litres of water weighs 3 kg (6.6 lb).
◆Plan food by hiking days. Reroutes make some marker intervals longer or shorter than 1 km.

What I carried

This was my setup for a 2026 thru-hike. Choose gear for your fit, season and route.

Pack and sleep

  • I carried a 55 L pack and a two-person tent for me and my dog.
  • I used a −7°C (20°F) sleeping bag, liner and inflatable pad. Hut bunks are wooden platforms.
  • My pad was heavy and loud on hut platforms. I would replace it with a lighter, quieter one.
  • I carried a tent because hut platforms are first-come. Check camping options for each planned stop.

Water and food

  • I carried containers with 3 L total capacity and filled them according to the next dry stretch.
  • Treat water from lakes, creeks and hut barrels. Carry a backup treatment method.
  • Some huts have no cache. Existing caches may be full or damaged. Bring your own food-storage setup.
  • Plan food by days and include one extra day. Confirm any resupply arrangement directly before leaving.

Clothing and feet

  • Quick-dry hiking layers, dry sleep layers, a rain shell and a thin insulating layer.
  • I carried two or three pairs of wool socks. Carry blister care and treat hot spots early.
  • I wore Merrell Moab 3 waterproof mids. Break in your footwear before the hike.
  • I used gloves in July and carried a swimsuit for lake swims.

Navigation and safety

  • Download offline maps before leaving signal and carry backup route information.
  • Much of the trail is under tree cover. Carry a battery bank and do not rely on solar charging.
  • Carry a headlamp, first-aid kit, repair kit and your own medications.
  • Carry bear spray where you can reach it and know how to use it. Bear spray and fuel cannot travel on a commercial flight, including checked baggage.
  • I used a watch to record my route. A satellite communicator can send an SOS without cellular service. Confirm its subscription is active before leaving.

Small items

  • Trekking poles, a quick-dry towel, trowel, hand sanitiser, sunscreen, insect repellent, lip balm and a hat.
  • Pack liner, dry food bag, cord, tape, blunt-tip scissors and a small sewing kit.
  • Use small amounts of biodegradable soap at least 60 m (200 ft) from lakes and streams.

Dog

  • Bring the dog’s food, water capacity, bowl, leash, sleep setup and first-aid supplies. Ask your vet about nutrition, medication and insect protection.
  • Trail Paws paw wax is the lick-safe paw protection I carried. Check paws during road and rock sections.
  • As of August 2026, BC Transit allows only small pets in a secure handheld carrier. Confirm each shuttle, taxi, boat and accommodation will accept your dog.

What I’d leave behind

I carried gaiters, a second cook pot, spare town clothes and a hammock. I did not use them.
