Getting to the trail

Reach Powell River by ferry or air. Reach Sarah Point by water taxi or arranged rough-road access.

The trail is near qathet / Powell River, British Columbia. Driving from Vancouver requires ferry crossings. Confirm each schedule with the operator. The official trail site keeps its own getting-here page and the official trail maps.

From Vancouver, by road

Two ferries and two drives, about 5–6 hours all in:

  • Horseshoe Bay → Langdale — Horseshoe Bay is in West Vancouver; Langdale is the bottom of the Sunshine Coast. The scheduled crossing is about 40 minutes. Reservations are available; check summer availability.
  • Drive Highway 101 up the lower Sunshine Coast to Earls Cove, about 80 minutes
  • Earls Cove → Saltery Bay — the second crossing, over Jervis Inlet to the Powell River side. The scheduled crossing is about 50 minutes. Reservations are unavailable; vehicles board first-come, first-served.
  • Saltery Bay to Powell River is a 30-minute drive — and the southern trailhead is right at the Saltery Bay side, so a finishing hiker just walks off the trail and onto the boat

Check the BC Ferries schedule and reserve the Horseshoe Bay–Langdale leg when available. Check current conditions on the day you travel.

From Vancouver Island

One ferry: Comox (Little River) → Powell River (Westview) — Comox is midway up Vancouver Island’s east coast. The crossing is about 85 minutes. Use the BC Ferries schedule page.

Check the current schedule before choosing a connection.

Flying in

Pacific Coastal Airlines flies Vancouver (YVR South Terminal) → Powell River. Check the current timetable before connecting onward.

Bear spray and fuel canisters cannot travel on a commercial flight, including checked baggage. Arrange to buy them after landing and confirm stock before relying on one store.

Reaching the northern trailhead

The trail starts at Sarah Point. Lund Water Taxi runs from Lund Harbour, about 30 minutes north of Powell River by road. Its scheduled boats serve Savary Island; a Sarah Point drop is on request, so book ahead: 604-483-9749. Confirm the landing point, tide limits, luggage and pet rules when booking.

There is rough road access part way via Malaspina Road if you’d rather walk in, but confirm the vehicle, road condition and final walking distance with the operator.

Getting around Powell River

BC Transit’s Powell River system covers town and some rural connections. A ride is $2.25 and a DayPASS is $4.50 as of August 2026. Small pets are allowed only in a secure hand-held carrier. Route 12 serves Lang Bay and some trips reach Saltery Bay; several marked trips do not run on Thursdays. Check the exact route and day before relying on it.

The official map

qPAWS publishes official overview and section maps. Carry backup route information.

The shape of the trip

A north-to-south trip starts at Sarah Point and finishes beside the Saltery Bay ferry terminal. A south-to-north trip needs a planned pickup from Sarah Point or another northern access.
