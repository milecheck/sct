Getting to the trail

Powell River has no road in from the rest of the world. Here’s every way to arrive.

From Vancouver, by road

Two ferries and two drives, about 5–6 hours all in:

  • Horseshoe Bay → Langdale — 40 minutes on the water. This is the leg worth reserving on summer weekends
  • Drive Highway 101 up the lower Sunshine Coast to Earls Cove, about 80 minutes
  • Earls Cove → Saltery Bay — 50 minutes. No reservations; it’s first come, and it waits for no one
  • Saltery Bay to Powell River is a 30-minute drive — and the southern trailhead is right at the Saltery Bay side, so a finishing hiker just walks off the trail and onto the boat

BC Ferries schedules and reservations — both legs are on one through-fare if you ask for it.

From Vancouver Island

One ferry: Comox (Little River) → Powell River (Westview), about 90 minutes, a few sailings a day. Same BC Ferries schedule page.

Flying in

Pacific Coastal Airlines flies Vancouver (YVR South Terminal) → Powell River in about 35 minutes, several times a day.

What can’t fly with you: bear spray and butane canisters. Buy both when you land — Powell River Outdoors is next to the Westview ferry terminal and stocks both, plus freeze-dried meals ($15–23 each there, so order ahead in bulk if you’re counting dollars).

Reaching the northern trailhead

The trail starts at Sarah Point, and the classic way in is by water: the Lund water taxi runs from Lund harbour, about 30 minutes north of Powell River by road. Book ahead in summer.

There is rough road access part way via Malaspina Road if you’d rather walk in, but the boat drop at the point is how most thru-hikes begin.

Getting around Powell River

BC Transit’s Powell River system covers town, the rural connections, and an on-demand service. $2.25 a ride, $4.50 for a day pass — and you can just tap a debit card or Apple Pay on the bus, or use the Umo app. Two catches: no pets, and no Thursday service — check the schedule before you plan around it.

The official map

qPAWS publishes the official Sunshine Coast Trail maps — overview and section maps, plus the printed map sold locally. Buying the paper one funds the volunteers who build the huts and cut the deadfall. Carry it; batteries die.

The shape of the trip

Most people walk north to south: water taxi to Sarah Point, 180 km later step off at Saltery Bay straight onto the ferry home. No car shuttle, no backtracking — the trail ends at the boat.
